# 3. Writing the lead-in

The lead-in is the question itself. It should be a **closed, direct question**
that makes the task unmistakable.

## 3.1 Good lead-ins

* Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?
* Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?
* Which of the following is the most likely mechanism of this adverse effect?
* Which of the following laboratory findings is most likely in this patient?

## 3.2 Rules

* **Phrase it positively.** Avoid *NOT*, *EXCEPT*, *FALSE*, *LEAST likely*.
  Negative lead-ins measure the ability to spot a false statement, invert the
  usual reasoning and are a well-known source of errors among strong examinees.
* **Use "most"** (most likely, most appropriate) when several options could be
  defensible in some circumstance; the key must be the *best* answer.
* **Avoid incomplete sentences** that the options complete; they create
  grammatical cues and break the cover-the-options rule.
* **Ask one thing.** "Which is the diagnosis and the treatment?" is two items.

## 3.3 Rewriting a negative lead-in

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| All of the following are risk factors for deep vein thrombosis EXCEPT: | A 45-year-old woman … Which of the following most increases her risk of deep vein thrombosis? |
| Which drug should NOT be given to this patient? | Which of the following drugs is most likely to worsen this patient's condition? |
