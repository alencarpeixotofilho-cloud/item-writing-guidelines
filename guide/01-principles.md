# 1. Principles of a good multiple-choice item

A multiple-choice item is a measurement instrument. Its only job is to separate
examinees who have the intended knowledge or skill from those who do not —
nothing else should influence the response.

## 1.1 One item, one important idea

Each item should test **one clearly defined, important concept** taken from the
test blueprint. Avoid trivia, "gotcha" details and facts that a competent
professional would look up rather than memorize.

> **Ask yourself:** *Would a competent graduate be expected to know this, and
> does it matter for practice?*

## 1.2 Test application, not recall

Items that present a realistic situation and ask for a decision (diagnosis,
next step, mechanism, interpretation) measure higher-order thinking and are
more discriminating than isolated recall questions.

| Cognitive level | Typical lead-in |
|---|---|
| Recall | "Which of the following is the definition of …?" |
| Application | "Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?" |
| Analysis | "Which of the following best explains the change in …?" |

## 1.3 The "cover-the-options" rule

A well-written item can be answered by a knowledgeable examinee **with the
options covered**. If you need to read the options to understand what is
being asked, the stem or lead-in is incomplete.

## 1.4 Construct-irrelevant variance

Anything that changes the probability of a correct answer *for reasons other
than the construct* is a flaw. The two big families are:

* **Cues that help test-wise examinees** — grammatical agreement, the longest
  option being correct, repeated words, absolute terms in distractors.
* **Obstacles that hurt knowledgeable examinees** — ambiguous wording,
  negative phrasing, unnecessary complexity, tricky distractors.

Chapter [5](05-technical-flaws.md) catalogs both families.

## 1.5 Fairness and accessibility

Use plain, international language; avoid regional slang, idioms and
culturally specific references unless they are part of the construct. Use
generic drug names and standard units.
