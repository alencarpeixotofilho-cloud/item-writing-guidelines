## Stem
A 58-year-old man comes to the emergency department 40 minutes after the onset of crushing substernal chest pain radiating to the jaw, with diaphoresis and nausea. He has hypertension and smokes one pack of cigarettes daily. Blood pressure is 96/60 mm Hg and pulse is 52/min. The electrocardiogram shows ST-segment elevation in leads II, III and aVF, with reciprocal ST depression in leads I and aVL.

## Lead-in
Occlusion of which of the following arteries is the most likely cause of these findings?

## Options
A. Left anterior descending artery
B. Left circumflex artery
C. Left main coronary artery
D. Right coronary artery
E. First diagonal branch

## Key
D

## Stem
A 24-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department because of three days of polyuria, polydipsia, abdominal pain and vomiting. She has lost 4 kg over the past month. Pulse is 118/min, respirations are 28/min and deep, and blood pressure is 98/62 mm Hg. Mucous membranes are dry. Laboratory studies show a serum glucose of 380 mg/dL, arterial pH of 7.18, serum bicarbonate of 10 mEq/L, serum potassium of 4.6 mEq/L and positive serum ketones.

## Lead-in
Which of the following is the most appropriate initial step in management?

## Options
A. Intravenous 0.9% sodium chloride
B. Intravenous sodium bicarbonate
C. Subcutaneous insulin glargine
D. Oral metformin
E. Intravenous 5% dextrose in water

## Key
A
